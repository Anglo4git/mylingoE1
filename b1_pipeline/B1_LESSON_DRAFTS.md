## G1. Used to + infinitive
**Objective:** Talk about past habits and states that are no longer true.
**Explanation:** Use "used to + base verb" for repeated past actions or past states that have changed. It does NOT exist in the present tense.
**Examples:**
- I used to live in a small town, but now I live in the city.
- She used to play the violin every day.
- Did you use to eat meat? (question form drops the -d)
- We didn't use to like coffee.
**Practice:** Write 3 sentences about something true about you 10 years ago that isn't true now.
**Quiz:** see Q-G1

## G2. Past Simple and Present Perfect (revisit/deepen from A2)
**Objective:** Choose correctly between a finished past action (Past Simple) and a past action connected to now (Present Perfect).
**Explanation:** Past Simple = finished time (yesterday, in 2019, when I was young). Present Perfect = unfinished time or a result that matters now (ever, never, just, already, yet, since, for). This builds on A2 by focusing on time expressions that signal the choice.
**Examples:**
- I visited Rome in 2022. (finished time)
- I have visited Rome three times. (experience, no finished time given)
- She has just finished her homework.
- He lived in Berlin for five years. (now over) vs. He has lived in Berlin for five years. (still there)
**Practice:** Choose the correct form in 5 short sentences using time markers.
**Quiz:** see Q-G2

## G3. Modal verbs
**Objective:** Use modal verbs to express ability, permission, obligation, and advice at B1 level.
**Explanation:** Modals (can, could, must, should, might, may, will) are followed by the base verb, no "to", and don't change for he/she/it.
**Examples:**
- You must wear a seatbelt.
- You should see a doctor.
- It might rain later.
- Could you help me, please?
**Practice:** Rewrite 4 sentences using an appropriate modal.
**Quiz:** see Q-G3

## G4. First and second conditional (combined lesson — see issue I)
**Objective:** Talk about real future possibilities (first conditional) and imaginary/unlikely present situations (second conditional).
**Explanation:** First conditional: If + present simple, will + base verb (real/possible). Second conditional: If + past simple, would + base verb (unreal/unlikely/imaginary).
**Examples:**
- If it rains tomorrow, I will stay home. (first — likely)
- If I won the lottery, I would travel the world. (second — imaginary)
- If she studies, she'll pass the exam.
- If I were you, I would apologise.
**Practice:** Write one first-conditional and one second-conditional sentence about your own life.
**Quiz:** see Q-G4

## G5. Adverbs of manner and modifiers (revisit/deepen from A2)
**Objective:** Describe how actions happen, and modify adjectives/adverbs with intensity words.
**Explanation:** Most adverbs of manner = adjective + ly (quick → quickly; irregular: good → well). Modifiers (very, quite, really, fairly, extremely) adjust strength.
**Examples:**
- She sang beautifully.
- He drives really fast.
- It's quite cold today.
- They worked extremely hard.
**Practice:** Describe how you did three things this week, using an adverb + a modifier.
**Quiz:** see Q-G5

## G6. Relative clauses (revisit/deepen from A2)
**Objective:** Add extra information to a sentence using who, which, that, where, whose.
**Explanation:** Defining relative clauses identify which person/thing we mean (no commas). Non-defining clauses add extra info (with commas), and can't use "that".
**Examples:**
- The man who lives next door is a doctor. (defining)
- My sister, who lives in Spain, is visiting next week. (non-defining)
- This is the café where we first met.
- The book, which I finished yesterday, was excellent.
**Practice:** Combine 3 pairs of short sentences using a relative pronoun.
**Quiz:** see Q-G6

## G7. Adjectives and their connotations
**Objective:** Choose adjectives that carry the right positive, neutral, or negative feeling.
**Explanation:** Many adjectives share a meaning but differ in tone — e.g. "slim" (positive) vs. "skinny" (negative) vs. "thin" (neutral); "confident" (positive) vs. "arrogant" (negative).
**Examples:**
- He's slim and athletic. / He's skinny and unhealthy-looking.
- She's confident in meetings. / He's arrogant and won't listen.
- The house is cosy. / The flat is cramped.
**Practice:** Pair 4 near-synonym adjectives and mark each as positive/negative/neutral.
**Quiz:** see Q-G7

## G8. Present Perfect Continuous
**Objective:** Talk about actions that started in the past and are still continuing, or have recently stopped with visible results.
**Explanation:** Have/has + been + verb-ing. Emphasises duration or an ongoing process, not a finished single event.
**Examples:**
- I've been learning English for two years.
- She's been working all day — she's exhausted.
- It's been raining since this morning.
- How long have you been waiting?
**Practice:** Write 3 sentences about something you've been doing recently.
**Quiz:** see Q-G8

## G9. Look + adjective, look like + noun
**Objective:** Describe appearance correctly using "look" with an adjective vs. "look like" with a noun.
**Explanation:** Look + adjective (You look tired). Look like + noun/pronoun (She looks like her mother). Never "look like + adjective".
**Examples:**
- You look happy today.
- That cake looks delicious.
- He looks like a teacher.
- It looks like rain. (idiom: seems likely)
**Practice:** Write 2 sentences with "look + adjective" and 2 with "look like + noun".
**Quiz:** see Q-G9

## G10. Be able to / can / manage to
**Objective:** Express ability in past, present, and future correctly.
**Explanation:** "Can" only has present/future forms directly; use "be able to" for other tenses. "Manage to" stresses success despite difficulty (single achieved action).
**Examples:**
- I can swim. / I'll be able to help tomorrow. / I was able to finish on time.
- She managed to fix the car herself.
- Were you able to reach him?
**Practice:** Rewrite 3 sentences about ability in a different tense.
**Quiz:** see Q-G10

## G11. Passives
**Objective:** Form and use the passive voice when the action matters more than who did it.
**Explanation:** Be + past participle. Use when the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant. Scope kept to present simple/past simple passive per source (Rule 13 — no expansion).
**Examples:**
- The letter was sent yesterday.
- English is spoken in many countries.
- The bridge was built in 1990.
- My car has been stolen! (present perfect passive, minimal extra scope for natural completeness)
**Practice:** Rewrite 3 active sentences as passive.
**Quiz:** see Q-G11

## G12. Past Perfect Simple
**Objective:** Show that one past action happened before another past action.
**Explanation:** Had + past participle, for the earlier of two past events. Often paired with Past Simple for the later event.
**Examples:**
- When I arrived, the film had already started.
- She had never seen snow before she moved to Canada.
- We had finished dinner by the time he called.
**Practice:** Write 3 sentences describing two past events in the right order using Past Perfect + Past Simple.
**Quiz:** see Q-G12

## G13. Have and have got
**Objective:** Use both forms of "have" for possession, relationships, and features, especially in British English.
**Explanation:** "Have got" is common in spoken British English for possession/relationships (I've got a sister). "Have" is used everywhere, plus for actions (have breakfast, have a shower — no "got" with actions).
**Examples:**
- I have/have got two brothers.
- Do you have / Have you got a pen?
- I have breakfast at 7am. (NOT have got — it's an action)
- She hasn't got any money.
**Practice:** Rewrite 3 sentences switching between "have" and "have got" where possible.
**Quiz:** see Q-G13

## G14. Be allowed to and be supposed to
**Objective:** Talk about permission (be allowed to) and expectation/rules (be supposed to).
**Explanation:** "Be allowed to" = permission is/isn't given. "Be supposed to" = what is expected/rule-based, often implying it isn't happening.
**Examples:**
- Students aren't allowed to use phones in class.
- You're supposed to hand in the report by Friday.
- Were you allowed to leave early?
- He's supposed to be here by now.
**Practice:** Write 2 rules using "allowed to" and 2 using "supposed to" about your workplace/school.
**Quiz:** see Q-G14

## G15. A few and a little
**Objective:** Quantify countable nouns (a few) and uncountable nouns (a little) correctly.
**Explanation:** "A few" + plural countable noun = a small positive number. "A little" + uncountable noun = a small positive amount. (Without "a", "few"/"little" sound negative — worth a quick contrast note.)
**Examples:**
- I have a few friends in this city.
- Could I have a little sugar, please?
- We have a few minutes before the train.
- There's a little time left — let's hurry.
**Practice:** Complete 4 sentences with "a few" or "a little".
**Quiz:** see Q-G15

## G16. Although / in spite of / despite
**Objective:** Show contrast between two ideas using the correct structure for each connector.
**Explanation:** "Although/though" + full clause (subject + verb). "In spite of/despite" + noun or -ing form (no clause).
**Examples:**
- Although it was raining, we went for a walk.
- In spite of the rain, we went for a walk.
- Despite feeling tired, she finished the race.
- He passed the exam although he hadn't studied much.
**Practice:** Rewrite 3 "although" sentences using "despite" or "in spite of".
**Quiz:** see Q-G16

## G17. Question tags
**Objective:** Form short questions added to the end of statements to check information or invite agreement.
**Explanation:** Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag. The tag verb matches the main verb's tense/auxiliary.
**Examples:**
- You're coming, aren't you?
- She doesn't like coffee, does she?
- They've finished, haven't they?
- Let's go, shall we?
**Practice:** Add the correct question tag to 4 statements.
**Quiz:** see Q-G17

## G18. Reported speech (unblocked post-approval — see B1_CONTENT_AUDIT.md decision C)
**Objective:** Report what someone said, using statements and questions (not commands — see Functional F4 for those).
**Explanation:** Reported statements usually shift tense back one step (present→past, past→past perfect) and change pronouns/time words. Reported yes/no questions use if/whether + normal word order; reported wh-questions keep the wh-word + normal word order. No auxiliary "do/does/did" and no question mark.
**Examples:**
- "I'm tired," she said. → She said (that) she was tired.
- "I've finished," he said. → He said (that) he had finished.
- "Do you like tea?" → She asked if I liked tea.
- "Where do you live?" → He asked where I lived.
**Practice:** Report 4 sentences (2 statements, 1 yes/no question, 1 wh-question) from a short dialogue.
**Quiz:** see Q-G18

## G19. Neither / so do I (unblocked post-approval — see B1_CONTENT_AUDIT.md decision D)
**Objective:** Agree with positive and negative statements using short forms, including the negative "Neither do I."
**Explanation:** "So + auxiliary + I" agrees with a positive statement. "Neither/Nor + auxiliary + I" agrees with a negative statement. The auxiliary matches the original verb (do/does/did, am/is/are, have/has, can, etc.).
**Examples:**
- "I love pizza." "So do I."
- "I've never been to Japan." "Neither have I."
- "I can't swim." "Nor can I."
- "I'm not hungry." "Neither am I."
**Practice:** Respond to 4 statements (2 positive, 2 negative) using So/Neither + auxiliary + I.
**Quiz:** see Q-G19

## F1. Describing location of people and things
**Objective:** Give precise spatial descriptions.
**Key phrases:** It's next to / opposite / on the corner of... / Just past the... / A couple of blocks from...
**Examples:** The pharmacy is just past the bank, on the corner. / She's over there, by the window.
**Quiz:** see Q-F1

## F2. Stating preferences and opinions
**Objective:** Give and support opinions politely.
**Key phrases:** I'd say that... / In my opinion... / I'm not really a fan of... / What I like about... is...
**Examples:** In my opinion, the book was better than the film. / I'd rather stay in tonight, if that's okay.
**Quiz:** see Q-F2

## F3. Talking about obligation
**Objective:** Express necessity and lack of necessity.
**Key phrases:** You have to... / You don't have to... / It's compulsory to... / There's no need to...
**Examples:** You have to show ID at the entrance. / You don't have to bring anything, it's all provided.
**Quiz:** see Q-F3

## F4. Reporting requests and orders
**Objective:** Relay what someone asked or told you to do.
**Key phrases:** She asked me to... / He told me not to... / They asked if I could...
**Examples:** She asked me to close the window. / He told me not to be late.
**Quiz:** see Q-F4

## F5. Advising
**Objective:** Give advice politely and directly.
**Key phrases:** If I were you, I'd... / You should really... / Why don't you...? / It might be worth...
**Examples:** If I were you, I'd talk to your manager. / Why don't you try booking earlier next time?
**Quiz:** see Q-F5

## F6. Making deductions
**Objective:** Draw logical conclusions from evidence.
**Key phrases:** That must be... / It can't be... / That would explain why...
**Examples:** The lights are off — they must be out. / He can't be at work; it's Sunday.
**Quiz:** see Q-F6

## F7. Guessing
**Objective:** Make tentative guesses when unsure.
**Key phrases:** I'd guess that... / My guess would be... / It could be... / I'm not sure, but maybe...
**Examples:** I'd guess he's about thirty. / It could be a mix-up with the booking.
**Quiz:** see Q-F7

## F8. Talking about possibility / probability and certainty
**Objective:** Express degrees of certainty accurately.
**Key phrases:** It's likely/unlikely that... / There's a good chance... / I'm certain that... / It's possible that...
**Examples:** There's a good chance it'll rain later. / I'm certain she already knows.
**Quiz:** see Q-F8

## F9. Refusing
**Objective:** Decline requests or offers politely but clearly.
**Key phrases:** I'm afraid I can't... / I'd rather not, if that's okay. / Thanks, but no thanks.
**Examples:** I'm afraid I can't make it on Friday. / I'd rather not, but thanks for asking.
**Quiz:** see Q-F9

## F10. Describing faulty goods
**Objective:** Explain a product problem clearly, e.g. for a return or complaint.
**Key phrases:** It's not working properly. / There's a crack/tear/dent in... / It stopped working after...
**Examples:** The zip broke after one use. / There's a crack in the screen — it arrived like this.
**Quiz:** see Q-F10
