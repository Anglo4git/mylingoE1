## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-10 — Present Simple
**Objective:** Use the present simple for facts, habits and routines.
**Explanation:** The present simple describes things that are generally
true or that happen regularly. Add -s/-es for he/she/it. Use don't/
doesn't for negatives and questions.
**Examples:**
- She works in a hospital.
- We don't eat meat.
- Does he live near here?
- The shop opens at nine.
**Practice:** Complete 5 sentences about a daily routine, choosing the
correct verb form (base or -s).
**Quiz connection:** 5-question sample quiz drilling third-person -s,
negatives with don't/doesn't, and question formation with do/does.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-11 — Past Simple
**Objective:** Talk about finished actions and events in the past.
**Explanation:** Regular verbs add -ed; many common verbs are
irregular. Use didn't for negatives and did for questions (base verb
after did/didn't).
**Examples:**
- I visited my grandparents last weekend.
- She went to Paris in June.
- We didn't watch the film.
- Did you call him yesterday?
**Practice:** Rewrite 5 present-simple sentences in the past simple,
including 2 irregular verbs.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz mixing regular/irregular past
forms and did/didn't questions.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-12 — Reflexive Pronouns
**Objective:** Use reflexive pronouns (myself, yourself, himself,
herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves) correctly.
**Explanation:** Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and object of
a verb are the same person or thing.
**Examples:**
- I cut myself while cooking.
- She taught herself Spanish.
- They enjoyed themselves at the party.
- Be careful — you might hurt yourself.
**Practice:** Match 5 sentences to the correct reflexive pronoun.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz on pronoun/subject agreement.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-13 — So / Such
**Objective:** Use so and such to add emphasis to adjectives and nouns.
**Explanation:** Use "so" directly before an adjective or adverb ("so
tired"). Use "such" before a noun, or an adjective + noun ("such a
long day").
**Examples:**
- The film was so boring.
- It's such a beautiful city.
- She speaks so quickly.
- They are such good friends.
**Practice:** Choose "so" or "such" to complete 5 sentences.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz distinguishing so vs. such by
what follows (adjective alone vs. noun phrase).

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-14 — Have to / Need to (Obligation)
**Objective:** Express obligation and necessity.
**Explanation:** "Have to" and "need to" both show something is
necessary. Use don't have to / don't need to for "not necessary"
(different meaning from "must not").
**Examples:**
- I have to finish this report today.
- You don't need to bring anything.
- Does she have to work on Saturdays?
- We need to leave before 8am.
**Practice:** Write 5 sentences about rules at work or school using
have to / need to.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz, including the don't have to vs.
mustn't contrast.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-15 — Present Simple Passive
**Objective:** Form and use the present simple passive.
**Explanation:** Use am/is/are + past participle when the focus is on
the action or the receiver, not who does it.
**Examples:**
- English is spoken in many countries.
- The letters are delivered every morning.
- Coffee is grown in Brazil.
- This bridge was built... (flagged as past — kept out; present only
  in this lesson).
**Practice:** Rewrite 5 active sentences in the passive.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz on am/is/are + past participle
formation.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-16 — When / While
**Objective:** Use when and while to connect two actions in time.
**Explanation:** "While" is often used with a longer, ongoing action
(often past continuous); "when" often introduces a shorter action that
interrupts or follows.
**Examples:**
- While I was cooking, the phone rang.
- When she arrived, we started the meeting.
- He read a book while he waited.
- When it rains, I take an umbrella.
**Practice:** Combine 5 pairs of sentences using when or while.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz choosing when vs. while in
context.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-17 — Must / Might for Deductions
**Objective:** Make logical deductions about the present.
**Explanation:** Use "must" when you're almost certain something is
true; use "might" (or "may"/"could") when it's only possible.
**Examples:**
- The lights are off — they must be out.
- She might be at work; I'm not sure.
- He must be tired after that trip.
- It might rain later.
**Practice:** Choose must or might for 5 short situations.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz on certainty (must) vs.
possibility (might).

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-18 — As Soon As
**Objective:** Use "as soon as" to talk about one action happening
immediately after another.
**Explanation:** "As soon as" introduces a time clause; the main clause
often uses will/going to for a future result.
**Examples:**
- I'll call you as soon as I land.
- As soon as she gets home, she checks her email.
- We'll start as soon as everyone arrives.
- He left as soon as the meeting ended.
**Practice:** Complete 5 sentences with a clause starting "as soon as".
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz on clause order and tense choice.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-19 — Be Able To / Good At
**Objective:** Talk about ability and skill.
**Explanation:** "Be able to" describes ability (can be used in more
tenses than "can"); "good at" + noun/gerund describes skill level.
**Examples:**
- I wasn't able to finish on time.
- She's very good at cooking.
- Will you be able to come tomorrow?
- He's good at explaining things clearly.
**Practice:** Write 5 sentences about your own abilities.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz mixing be able to (tense forms)
and good at + -ing.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-20 — Although / However
**Objective:** Show contrast between two ideas.
**Explanation:** "Although" joins two clauses in one sentence.
"However" usually starts a new sentence and is followed by a comma.
**Examples:**
- Although it was raining, we went for a walk.
- The trip was long. However, it was worth it.
- She passed the test although she didn't study much.
- He's friendly. However, he's also very shy.
**Practice:** Rewrite 5 pairs of contrasting sentences using although
or however.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz on punctuation and clause
position.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-21 — Used To
**Objective:** Talk about past habits and states that are no longer
true.
**Explanation:** "Used to" + base verb describes a repeated past action
or state that has changed. Negative: didn't use to.
**Examples:**
- I used to live in Rome.
- She didn't use to like coffee.
- Did you use to play an instrument?
- We used to walk to school every day.
**Practice:** Write 5 sentences about how things were different when
you were younger.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz on used to vs. plain past simple.

## course-a2-unit-01-lesson-22 — Relative Clauses
**Objective:** Add extra information about a noun using who, which,
that, where.
**Explanation:** "Who" for people, "which" for things, "that" for
either (informal), "where" for places.
**Examples:**
- The woman who called you is my sister.
- This is the book that I told you about.
- We visited the town where I was born.
- The car, which is new, is parked outside.
**Practice:** Combine 5 pairs of sentences into one using a relative
pronoun.
**Quiz connection:** 5-question quiz choosing the correct relative
pronoun for people/things/places.
